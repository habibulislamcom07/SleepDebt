Why do I feel groggy after a nap?

If you wake up from a nap feeling worse than before, you are experiencing Sleep Inertia. This happens when you wake up during deep sleep (usually between 30 to 60 minutes into a nap). The best way to avoid this is by using a Nap Time Calculator to plan either a short 20-minute Power Nap or a full 90-minute sleep cycle.

The Science of the Perfect Nap

Our bodies sleep in cycles of roughly 90 minutes.
• 20 Minutes: You stay in light sleep (Stage 1 & 2), waking up refreshed.
• 90 Minutes: You complete a full cycle including REM sleep, which is great for creativity and memory.
• Avoid 45-60 Minutes: You enter slow-wave (deep) sleep, making it very hard to wake up without feeling groggy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a power nap?

A power nap is a short sleep session of about 15 to 20 minutes. It ends before you enter deep sleep, providing a quick boost of alertness and energy without the grogginess.

What is the best time of day to take a nap?

The best time is usually between 1:00 PM and 3:00 PM. This is when your body naturally experiences a dip in energy (circadian rhythm) and it is far enough from bedtime to not disrupt your night sleep.

How do I wake up from a 20-minute nap?

Set an alarm for exactly 25 minutes (5 minutes to fall asleep + 20 minutes of sleep) so you wake up during the light sleep phase. Also, keeping the room bright helps you wake up faster.
